LoonInspect's second major release: vulnerability intelligence an organization earns by sharing
or pays for without sharing (lands with the promote PRs), a second sign-in factor, a PostgreSQL you
run, and published images, with a schema that records which releases can read it.

## What ships

### Vulnerability intelligence, by contribution or payment (#620)

- **Paid access, without sharing inventory.** Settings › Intelligence Access activates an
  organization with a one-time `loon_act_` secret from LoonSec support, refreshes the corpus once
  a day or on demand, rotates the credential and disconnects locally (#622). A paid request
  carries the credential, the client version and the channel, and nothing from the fleet. Paying
  never turns sharing on, a connection's stored licence value is never sent, and nothing is sent
  until an administrator activates. On by default (lands with the promote PRs).
- **Contribution receipts, for an organization that shares.** A consenting exchange asks for a
  receipt, and an accepted upload earns one, good for 30 days; the newest is kept. When a day's
  exchange brings no corpus, the receipt fetches it without uploading again. Turning sharing off
  stops uploads at once and withdraws the receipt at the service (#622). On by default (lands
  with the promote PRs).
- **Each organization keeps the corpus release it selected.** A release is selected only after
  the organization's assessment pass succeeds. Expiry, an outage or withdrawn consent leaves the
  held release answering under its own date, and an old or incomplete answer never reads as a
  fix. The current and previous selections are kept for rollback, each reassessment is recorded
  in Devices › Device history, and cleanup is a command an operator runs (#621). On by default
  (lands with the promote PRs).
- **The exchange reaches LoonSec's production collector.** `SHARING_ENDPOINT` defaults to
  `https://api.next.loonsec.io/v1/exchange` and `INTELLIGENCE_ENDPOINT` to
  `https://api.next.loonsec.io`. v1.0.0's default collector address answered every exchange with
  `403`, so step 2 of *Upgrading from v1.0.0* is a decision to make before you update.
- **The legacy exchange has no retirement date.** A sharing organization keeps its tier,
  exclusions and daily exchange, with receipts or without. A date will be published in
  [docs/data-sharing.md](https://github.com/LoonSecIO/LoonInspect/blob/v2.0.0/docs/data-sharing.md#the-contributor-transition-v2)
  at least 30 days before it takes effect, and nothing counts down on the instance (#624).
- **Reveals stay paused.** This build stores and answers no reveal request at any tier and drops
  any a v1.0.0 build queued. The `reveal` tier stays as set and uploads what `keys` uploads (#624).
- **Request coverage and Report an incorrect match** are built and ship off; see *What stays off*.

### Two-step sign-in (#653)

- A local account adds an authenticator-app code (TOTP) under *Two-step sign-in* on My Account,
  with ten one-use recovery codes.
- Settings › Accounts sets who must have one: nobody (the default), administrators, or everyone.
  The break-glass account is never asked. An administrator can remove another account's second
  factor.
- The audit log records enrolment, refused codes, recovery-code use and replacement, removal and
  policy changes.

### A PostgreSQL you run (#654)

`DATABASE_MODE=external` with `docker-compose.external.yml` runs the app against your server. The
URL must ask for TLS (`?ssl=require`, `verify-ca` or `verify-full`), and before it migrates the app
refuses a superuser, a `BYPASSRLS` role, or a role that cannot create in schema `public`, each in
a sentence. `looninspect-db-init` prepares the application role; two app containers take turns on
the migration. [operations.md §8](https://github.com/LoonSecIO/LoonInspect/blob/v2.0.0/docs/operations.md)
has backup, restore, upgrade and rollback for this shape.

### Published images, and a schema that says which releases can read it (#655, #672)

- `ghcr.io/loonsecio/looninspect:v2.0.0` and `ghcr.io/loonsecio/looninspect-db:v2.0.0`, for
  `linux/amd64` and `linux/arm64`, are the images `main` built for the release commit, copied at
  publication, never tagged `latest`. `docker-compose.pull.yml` runs them
  ([README step 4](https://github.com/LoonSecIO/LoonInspect/blob/v2.0.0/README.md#4-build-and-run)).
- The schema records the oldest release that can read it. An image that finds a revision it does
  not carry starts without migrating when its release is at or above that record, and otherwise
  refuses in one sentence naming both releases. So once a release follows this one, stepping back
  from it to v2.0.0 is an image swap. Stepping back from v2.0.0 to v1.0.0 is not: see
  *Rolling back*.
- Each release page carries its upgrade notes: the migrations the update runs at first start,
  and first, any that needs a maintenance window or free disk.

### Also since v1.0.0

- An extension attribute's value change is evidence for inventory summaries, gated by the Change
  Log policy. Summary events gain `extension_attributes` in `evidenceScope` and an
  `extensionAttributes` change section, additively (#644).
- The device page dates each event by when this instance recorded it, with the Mac's report
  time in the tooltip (#645). *Findings first checked* carries this instance's clock at that
  check (#646). Device history reads *← Older* and *Newer →* and pages that way (#618).
- The audit log gains `auth.mfa.*`, `settings.mfa_policy.changed`, `intelligence.*` and
  `submission.*` actions.
- `docker-compose.yml` passes every setting the app reads, each at its default under a comment
  naming the page that explains it (#707).
- A feature-flag toggle saved while a slow listing was loading is no longer overwritten by it (#675).
- [troubleshooting.md](https://github.com/LoonSecIO/LoonInspect/blob/v2.0.0/docs/troubleshooting.md)
  has eighteen paths, two-step sign-in (§20) and submissions (§21) among them, and step-throughs
  for paid access and contribution receipts.

## Upgrading from v1.0.0

1. **Back up first**: the dump and `ENCRYPTION_KEY`
   ([operations.md §1–2](https://github.com/LoonSecIO/LoonInspect/blob/v2.0.0/docs/operations.md)).
   The key stays the same, and the dump is the simplest way back.
2. **Decide about sharing before you update.** On v1.0.0's default address, `api.loonsec.io`, no
   exchange reached LoonSec: every one read `403 Missing Authentication Token`. v2.0.0 posts to
   the production collector, so an organization whose sharing is on sends its first exchange that
   arrives at its next daily run (or **Send now**), keys and install counts as its tier says, and
   the reply's corpus link is where Posture › Vulnerabilities starts answering from. Settings ›
   Data Sharing shows the literal next payload; choose **Off** before updating if it should not
   leave.
   With receipts on, that exchange also asks for a receipt, which the share log shows as
   `"participation_receipt": true` (lands with the promote PRs).
3. **Read the upgrade notes below.** Ten migrations run at the first start, in one transaction.
   `e621c4a8b903` rebuilds the installed-apps index while the instance answers nothing: a second
   or two on 600,000 installed apps, longer on a larger table. #624's walk from a v1.0.0 database
   of 10,000 devices with 60 apps each ran the whole chain in about 14 seconds and grew it by 1 MB.
   The migrations change no sharing tier, stored answer or licence value, and send nothing.
4. **Take the release.** `git fetch --tags && git checkout v2.0.0`, then build it with
   `GIT_SHA=$(git rev-parse --short HEAD) docker compose up -d --build`, which needs Docker's
   BuildKit builder (the default in Docker Desktop and Compose v2). Or pull it: add
   `COMPOSE_FILE=docker-compose.yml:docker-compose.pull.yml` and `LOONINSPECT_VERSION=v2.0.0`
   to `.env`, then `docker compose pull && docker compose up -d` (Compose 2.24 or newer).
5. **Check `.env`.** v1.0.0's `docker-compose.yml` passed 26 fewer settings than this one. Six
   are new: `INTELLIGENCE_ACCESS`, `CONTRIBUTION_RECEIPTS`, `VULN_TENANT_SELECTION`,
   `VULN_RELEASE_RETENTION`, `INTELLIGENCE_SUBMISSIONS` and `INTELLIGENCE_ENDPOINT`. The other
   twenty, among them `LOG_LEVEL`, `SYNC_TIMEZONE`, `SHARING_ENDPOINT` and the retention days,
   existed and ran at their defaults, as they still do (`SHARING_ENDPOINT`'s is step 2). A line
   for one of them in `.env`, which v1.0.0 ignored, takes effect now, and a value the app refuses
   stops the start with a sentence naming the variable
   ([troubleshooting.md §4](https://github.com/LoonSecIO/LoonInspect/blob/v2.0.0/docs/troubleshooting.md)
   step 2). `SYNC_HOUR`, `SYNC_MINUTE` and `SYNC_TIMEZONE` are among the values checked now.

## What stays off, and why

**Request coverage and Report an incorrect match** (#623) ship with `INTELLIGENCE_SUBMISSIONS`
off, as ruled on 2026-09-27 (#692). With it off, Posture › Vulnerabilities offers neither, and
nothing is previewed or sent; a case already sent can still be refreshed and withdrawn. Each also
needs paid access on. They stay off because the half that answers them is not live yet: LoonSec's
review and publication of a submission (LoonVD-Internal #36) and a walk of one submission through
to publication come after v2.0.0, and the production service does not accept submissions yet.
The default flips on once both are true, and the switch goes one release later.

## Rolling back

**To v1.0.0 it is still the downgrade**
([KNOWN_ISSUES.md §6](https://github.com/LoonSecIO/LoonInspect/blob/v2.0.0/KNOWN_ISSUES.md)).
v1.0.0 predates the schema's release record, so starting it on a database v2.0.0 migrated
crash-loops on `Can't locate revision identified by '…'`. Restore the dump taken before the
upgrade beside the v1.0.0 image
([operations.md §3](https://github.com/LoonSecIO/LoonInspect/blob/v2.0.0/docs/operations.md)),
or, with v2.0.0 still running, run the downgrade to v1.0.0's head (`f526e8b3d901`) from the
v2.0.0 image first:

```bash
docker compose run --rm --no-deps app uv run --frozen --no-sync --no-dev alembic downgrade f526e8b3d901
git checkout v1.0.0
GIT_SHA=$(git rev-parse --short HEAD) docker compose up -d --build
```

- The downgrade runs in one transaction and removes what v2.0.0 added: second factors and the
  sign-in policy, the paid credential, the contribution receipt, submission cases, the retained
  corpus releases with each organization's selections, and the release record. It rebuilds the
  index `e621c4a8b903` replaced, so it wants the same window.
- Once an organization has recorded assessment history, it refuses: *Assessment history exists.
  Keep this schema or restore a pre-upgrade backup; downgrade would lose evidence.* The log lists
  the steps it ran before the refusal, and the transaction takes them all back: the database is
  still at v2.0.0's head, and the dump is the way back.
- On an install that pulls, delete the `COMPOSE_FILE` and `LOONINSPECT_VERSION` lines from `.env`
  before the checkout: v1.0.0 has no published image and no `docker-compose.pull.yml`.
- An instance on `DATABASE_MODE=external` has no v1.0.0 to return to: v1.0.0 refuses that mode.

**Back to v2.0.0 from a later release it is an image swap**, unless that release's notes say it
needs a later one ([operations.md §5](https://github.com/LoonSecIO/LoonInspect/blob/v2.0.0/docs/operations.md)).
Never edit `alembic_version` or `schema_release` to make an image start.

## Known limitations

- **Jamf Pro computers only.** Apple mobile devices are future work (#616); other MDMs are outside
  this release.
- **Retained intelligence grows, and cleanup is manual.** Additional acquired releases may be
  retired after 30 days, but only by the cleanup command, and assessment history is never pruned
  by it. At 10,000 devices with 100 apps each, a selection or rollback took 63–79 seconds on one
  Apple-silicon Mac and three transitions kept about 500 MB of assessment JSON; an organization's
  update holds that organization's lock until it commits, so its other writers wait. Concurrency,
  WAL and history sizing are v2.1 work
  ([KNOWN_ISSUES.md §9](https://github.com/LoonSecIO/LoonInspect/blob/v2.0.0/KNOWN_ISSUES.md)).
- **Paid access is operated by hand.** Activation secrets, renewals, extensions and trials come
  from LoonSec support; there is no automated billing or device metering.
- **The service runs on an interim name.** `api.next.loonsec.io` is the production service;
  `api.loonsec.io` is not yet, and an endpoint set to it reads `403` on every request.
- **A step back to v1.x is the downgrade above**, until a release after this one has shown the
  image swap.
- **Building from source needs BuildKit** (#716). The legacy builder (`DOCKER_BUILDKIT=0`) stops
  at parse time with `failed to parse platform`, a sentence troubleshooting.md does not quote yet.
- **`SYNC_TIMEZONE` takes canonical names only** (#713). The image carries no legacy aliases such
  as `US/Central`, and the refusal does not say so yet: use `America/Chicago` and its kind.
- **Single sign-on, WebAuthn and encryption-key rotation remain future work** (#303, #144).
  Two-step sign-in is TOTP on local accounts.
- **Unchanged from v1.0.0:** the change log and the observation ledger have no retention limit, a
  full database volume says so only in the database's log, Docker Compose does not restart a
  running app whose healthcheck fails, a wrong `ENCRYPTION_KEY` is found only when a credential is
  read, and the Overview's patch-laggards tile has a documented scaling limit (KNOWN_ISSUES.md
  §1–§5, §7).
